# Procedures
note for agent: ...

Procedures: mini-instructions of one or a few paragraphs, in human and not very concrete language, with no references to code or to the repo, readable by humans and machines alike. Each one has a `title` and `text` (a list of paragraphs).

## Solving a problem

Start by saying the problem in plain words, as if to someone who knows none of the tools. If it cannot be said simply, it is not understood yet: keep asking until it can.

Then look for the smallest useful answer. Every answer is a model and every model is wrong somewhere; pick the one that is useful enough, try it, and keep it only while it keeps being useful.

## Changing something

Before adding, look for what can be removed; before writing something new, look for what can be changed. Add only what neither covers.

Stop when the change gives most of its value. The last details cost the most and are worth the least; leave them for when they are needed.

## Describing something

Say what it is and what it is not, in a few words each. The two together draw its edge better than a long text on either side.

Keep only what carries most of the meaning. If a reader would lose nothing without a sentence, the sentence goes.

## Learning from a mistake

When the same mistake happens twice, write the smallest rule that would have prevented it, and put it where it will be read at the right moment.

Rules age. A rule that no longer prevents anything is noise: remove it, so the ones that matter stay visible.

## Acting under doubt

When a step cannot be undone, or it reaches other people, ask first. When it can be undone, prefer doing it and showing the result over asking.

Say plainly what was done, what was not, and why. A clear partial result is more useful than an unclear complete one.
